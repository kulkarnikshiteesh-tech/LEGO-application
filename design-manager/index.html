Systems thinking

One system across the whole range.

At Makerverse I set the platform standards the whole team designs within, so every product is cheaper to make, faster to ship and consistent in a child's hands.

2 mm MDF
One sheet material across every product. Less laser-cutting, bought in bulk.
4 colours
A fixed EVA-foam palette, so foam is bought by the block and nothing is bespoke.
1 standard
A single M4 fastener everywhere, simpler to source and to assemble.
Shared PCB
A reusable board stack across products. The boards wire together, which also teaches kids circuits.

No-code Robot

Pre-primary · computational thinking through play

A robot that builds computational thinking in 4–6 year olds. Children sequence directional steps with buttons and watch it route across a printed map. The game in the hero above is this mechanic. I own the concept, the interaction model and the physical design; electronics R&D, who report to me, own the board.

The hard design problem is orientation: keeping the cognitive load right for a child still learning to track a turn. We tested the first prototype in multiple kindergartens.

Makeable-DFM

A manufacturing-analysis web tool

I kept seeing the same gap in young designers: they could not read a part for manufacturability. So I built a tool that does it. Makeable analyses injection-moulded parts for moldability, undercuts and wall thickness, suggests materials, and estimates Indian tooling cost. 25+ revisions, built solo alongside AI.

The clearest proof of the technical depth this kind of role used to ask for: real application architecture, 3D computation and manufacturing logic, shipped as working software.

Tooliqa interior-capture system

A five-part connected product

One product made of five connected parts: a handheld scanning device, a computer-vision pipeline, a 3D editor, a project-management CRM and a client interface. I owned the device's industrial design and its single-hand interaction, and held the design coherent across all five so it read as one system.

Took the device from a 2 kg proof-of-principle to a 900 g working prototype while keeping the interaction model intact.
