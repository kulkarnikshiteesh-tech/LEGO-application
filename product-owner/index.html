02 · Test with kidsI change the product when kids tell me to.

Two products, two times the kids forced a change. Different problems, the same instinct: watch them play, then act on what you see.

Case ABluetooth speaker · Makerverse

Five kids broke it three days before launch, so I rebuilt it overnight.

Three days before a 15-kid summer workshop, I ran a pilot with 5 kids. The MDF living hinges all broke, because kids handle things hard. I redesigned it overnight, prototyped at the vendor the next morning, tested on the spot, then produced it for the workshop.

Case BMEEBO & PYBO · Next Education

The modular build was too hard for younger kids, so I split the robot in two by age.

We planned one Mindstorms-style kit on Raspberry Pi. The younger kids found the modular construction too hard to put together, so we split it by age: MEEBO (ages 7 to 10, a fixed shape with no modular building) and PYBO (ages 11 and up, modular). Both moved to micro:bit, cheaper than Pi and cheaper still at the combined volume, which let us map the CS curriculum to each age. Published at IIT Bombay, 2021.

03 · MakeableI build the products I need.

My junior designers kept getting manufacturing wrong, so I built the tool that fixes it. Makeable analyses a part for moldability, undercuts, wall thickness and India tooling cost. I built it solo with AI. It started on 4 March 2026 and I have made it better almost every day since: undercut detection alone took 25-plus revisions to get right, and the other features each took many more. It is backed by a database that logs every session, so the product tells me how it is actually used. It is live, free for now, and on its way to becoming a business of its own.

04 · The numbersI own the numbers, and I measure.

On Gobble Bot the biggest cost lever was the body material. Moving from all-MDF to a mix of EVA foam and MDF, MDF kept for the rigid core and EVA for the rest, cut the material cost, cut the laser-cutting time, and cut the weight, so both the unit cost and the per-box shipping came down. None of it took the play out.

05 · The platformI build the platform, not just the product.

To ship a range cheaply and reliably, I set one standard everyone designs to: 2mm MDF throughout, a fixed four-colour foam palette, a single M4 screw, and a reusable stack of common PCBs instead of a new board per product. The boards connect by wire, which standardises components and lets kids see the circuit they are building. Every sheet is engraved with a lot and revision number, so a complaint traces to the exact batch.

07 · Working on nowThe game up top is real. I am building it.

The No-code Robot is a pre-primary coding toy. Kids sequence directional steps and watch the robot route itself across a printed map, building computational thinking before they can type. It runs on an ESP32 and the characters are swappable skins. Right now it is a working prototype in testing: we are running it with nursery kids to evaluate the product. No-code coding robots are not a new category and this one is a team effort; what I own is the design and the play, how it feels in a child's hands and how they learn from it. My electronics R&D owns the board. The little game you played at the top of this page is this product's mechanic.

And yes, KIDO is the penguin. Now you know what you were really playing.
