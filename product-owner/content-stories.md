## Gate 2 — Deciding from real user behaviour (their word: "telemetry")
**Bluetooth speaker (early Makerverse product):** booked a 3-day summer workshop, 15 kids enrolled. Ran a pilot at a friend's place 3 days before with 5 kids. Original design used MDF living hinges; all 5 broke because kids couldn't handle MDF gently. Redesigned overnight, made a new prototype at the vendor next morning, assembled and tested on the spot, then produced for the workshop. **Has before/after images.**
→ Validate early, kill the design that fails, ship the fix.

**Makeable-DFM (live, his most literal telemetry example):** a DFM/cost web tool he built solo with AI. Built and modified **almost every day based on user feedback**. Backed by **Supabase**, which logs session details. This is the cleanest "data-driven, telemetry, iterate on real behaviour" proof for the PO role.

**Makeable facts (confirmed 2026-06-18):** started **4 March 2026**, improved almost daily since. The **25+ revisions figure is for undercut detection alone**, not the whole product; other features each took many more. Data-driven decision: because most users skip the industry field, he **added a quick path to pick your own material and skip the industry questions** entirely.

**Real Supabase stats (from sessions_stats.csv, 2026-06-17):** 55 parts uploaded; 40 completed analysis (73%); 30% of analysed parts had undercuts flagged; 13% hit a moldability warning. Most-requested production volume: 1,000 (38%), then 10,000 (24%). Top recommended material: PC (22%). Top part type: enclosure (15%); 71% leave industry unset (want a fast answer, not a form). Small but real, used in section 03.

**MEEBO & PYBO (Next Education):** originally planned one Mindstorms-style programmable kit on Raspberry Pi. Prototyped and tested with kids; joining Technic-style pieces into structures was too hard for younger kids. Split into two age-segmented products: MEEBO (ages 7 to 10, fixed shape, no modular construction) and PYBO (ages 11 and up, modular, thinner/lighter). Switched both to **micro:bit** (cheaper than Raspberry Pi, plus volume discount across both robots combined). Let them map the CS curriculum to age appropriately. Research published at IIT Bombay 2021.
→ User testing + roadmap segmentation + cost decision in one.

**Gobble Bot colour reversal (data-driven product decision, added 2026-06-17):** V1 was natural MDF with yellow accents. For V2 they moved to EVA foam and tried a sporty yellow-and-red look. After launch, **sales dropped** — Gobble Bot is a rugged, blocky/masculine robot and the sporty colours did not land. So V3 switched to **black EVA + MDF**, and **sales recovered**. A real sales-data-driven call (reads as telemetry/measure). Images V1/V2/V3 in the GobbleBot folder. Lives in section 04 ("I own the numbers, and I measure").

## Gate 4 — Change management / standardisation platform
Drove a standardisation platform across design + manufacturing at Makerverse:
- **2mm MDF** standard across all products (less laser-cutting time, bulk ordering).
- **Fixed 4-colour EVA foam palette** (foam buys in block quantity, so all EVA products are designed to use only the 4 standard colours).
- **One screw type (M4)** everywhere, for bulk ordering.
- **Reusable common-PCB stack** instead of a new board per product: boards connect to each other via wires. Standardises components AND the physical wiring teaches kids circuits (constraint turned into a play feature).
- **Traceability:** engrave lot number + design-revision number on every sheet, so customer complaints trace to the exact batch and problem.

PO framing: set a platform standard the whole team designs within — cuts cost, simplifies supply chain, builds in quality and traceability.

**People side (honest):** no real pushback — his team is interns/freshers who are there to learn. Adoption came through teaching the standard, not overcoming resistance. **Mentoring inexperienced designers is a career-long strength** (7 interns + 2 freelancers mentored; has mostly led junior teams). Frame change-leadership as setting a clear standard and bringing people along by teaching, NOT as agile/scrum change management.
